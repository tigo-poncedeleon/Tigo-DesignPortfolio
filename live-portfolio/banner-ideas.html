A bottom banner, six ways

Six ASCII fields, each one a different mechanism rather than a different skin on the same one. They are live — what you see is the code that would ship, running at the width it would ship at. Move the pointer across two of them; they answer.

Everything is drawn from tokens this site already owns, which is why the palette row below is the site's own palette row. Pick a hue and scroll: a banner that only works on Paper is not a banner this site can have.

colour
Ink, the grey ramp, and nothing else. The ember switch is off by default — see the note under each field.
type
The one genuinely new decision: a monospace. ui-monospace resolves to SF Mono, which is SF Pro's own companion, so it is a sibling of the stack rather than a guest.
motion
30fps, capped. None of them loops on a period you can catch, and none of them runs while off screen.
reduced motion
Each draws one frame and stops. The field is still a drawing when it holds still.

Your system asks for reduced motion, so every field below is holding a single frame. Turn it off to see them move.

Current

a flow field, drifting right

A few hundred invisible particles walk a noise field that is biased toward the right edge, and each one leaves a mark in a density buffer that fades a little every frame. The glyph a cell gets is chosen by how much has piled up in it, so the ramp from . to # is doing the work a greyscale would do. What you read is not the particles; it is their wake. Slow, wide, never quite the same twice.


      

      

      
Tigo Ponce de Leóntigoponcedeleon@gmail.com

    

  

  

    

      
mechanism
Particle advection through 3D value noise, with a decaying deposit buffer.

      
what it costs
One noise call per particle, not per cell. The cheapest of the six.

      
ember
Would land on the densest cells — the head of each streak. Reads as sparks in a current.

      
the risk
It is beautiful and it is also generic. This is the ASCII field everyone has seen.

    

  








  

    

      02
      
Wake

      
still, until you touch it

    

    
A real damped wave equation on the grid — every cell watches its four
    neighbours and the whole sheet rings. Nothing drives it but you: the pointer drops energy
    where it passes and the ripples spread, interfere, and die out. Left alone it lets a drop
    fall every second or two, so the banner is never dead, only quiet. Move your pointer
    across it.

  

  

    

      

      

      

      
Tigo Ponce de Leóntigoponcedeleon@gmail.com

    

  

  

    

      
mechanism
Finite-difference wave equation, aspect-corrected so a ripple is round on screen and not on the grid.

      
what it costs
Four array reads per cell per frame. No noise at all.

      
ember
Would mark the crests only. Honest use — the crest is the thing you are watching.

      
the risk
On a phone there is no pointer, so it falls back to the idle drops and loses its whole argument.

    

  








  

    

      03
      
Tide

      
the page rests on a waterline

    

    
Four sine waves at incommensurate frequencies plus a drift of noise
    give a horizon that never repeats. Above it is empty page; the line itself is drawn as
    ~, / or \ depending on which way it is falling; below it the density climbs
    with depth. The argument for this one is that it is the only field with a top edge, so
    the banner ends where the water begins rather than fading out into a haze.

  

  

    

      

      

      

      
Tigo Ponce de Leóntigoponcedeleon@gmail.com

    

  

  

    

      
mechanism
Summed sines and one noise octave for the waterline; depth ramp and a shimmer below it.

      
what it costs
One noise call per column for the line, one per cell for the shimmer. Middling.

      
ember
Would mark the waterline itself, one glyph thick. This is the most striking of the six with ember on — try it.

      
the risk
It means something. A sea at the foot of a portfolio is a metaphor, and the rest of the site does not make metaphors.

    

  








  

    

      04
      
Signature

      
the field gathers into the name, then lets go

    

    
The particles from Current, given somewhere to be. Each one owns a
    home cell sampled from the name rasterised into the grid, and once every twelve seconds
    they all go to it — staggered, so the mark assembles rather than snapping — hold for about
    three seconds, and disperse. The rest of the time it is a drifting mist and you would not
    guess it spells anything. The payoff is that the banner says who made the page without
    a line of type doing it.

  

  

    

      

      

      

      
Tigo Ponce de Leóntigoponcedeleon@gmail.com

    

  

  

    

      
mechanism
Text rasterised at 3× into a cell mask; particles lerp between a noise walk and their home cell.

      
what it costs
Same as Current plus one canvas raster on resize.

      
ember
Would light the mark only while it is held — the field goes warm for three seconds and then cools.

      
the risk
Turn the content row off: with it on, the name is in the banner twice. If you pick this, the footer loses its name line and keeps only the address.

    

  








  

    

      05
      
Contour

      
a heightfield, drawn as the lines of a map

    

    
A two-octave noise landscape drifts under the band, and a cell is drawn
    only where the terrain crosses one of five elevations. The glyph is the direction the
    contour runs — -, \, |, / — read off the gradient, so the lines are
    genuinely tangent to the surface rather than stamped on. This is the quietest of the six,
    and the one that looks the most like the rest of the site: hairlines, mostly white space,
    a structure you can see the reasoning of.

  

  

    

      

      

      

      
Tigo Ponce de Leóntigoponcedeleon@gmail.com

    

  

  

    

      
mechanism
Heightfield into a buffer, then isolines by the fractional part, with the glyph chosen from the gradient angle.

      
what it costs
Two noise calls per cell, then a gradient pass over the buffer. The most expensive, and still cheap.

      
ember
Nothing to put it on. This field has no hot spot, which is an argument in its favour — the ember rule stays at four.

      
the risk
It is so quiet that at 62% ink it can read as an artefact rather than a decision. It may want a hair more contrast.

    

  








  

    

      06
      
Smolder

      
heat coming off the bottom edge

    

    
An actual fluid step — density is seeded along the very bottom row and
    then advected by tracing each cell backwards along its velocity and reading what was there,
    which is the same semi-Lagrangian move a real solver makes. It rises because the velocity
    field says up, faster where it is dense, and it is torn sideways by noise on the way. The
    pointer pushes into it. This is the only one whose geometry needs the bottom of the
    page: it is rising off the edge of the document.

  

  

    

      

      

      

      
Tigo Ponce de Leóntigoponcedeleon@gmail.com

    

  

  

    

      
mechanism
Semi-Lagrangian advection of a density field with buoyancy, a noise velocity field, and a bottom-row source.

      
what it costs
One noise call and one bilinear sample per cell. Comparable to Contour.

      
ember
The natural home for it — the source row, where the heat is. Switch it on and this is a different object.

      
the risk
Without ember it is a grey haze and reads as less deliberate than the other five. This one is really asking for the fifth colour.

    

  







  
Where I'd go

  
Contour is the one that belongs to this site. It is built from lines, it
  has no hot spot so it does not reopen the ember question, it holds up on every palette
  because it is structure rather than tone, and it is the only one that still reads as a
  drawing when it is frozen for reduced motion. Signature is the one that will
  be remembered — but it costs you the name in the footer, and it is a set piece, which is a
  different kind of thing from a banner.

  
Whichever you pick, it would ship as one css/banner.css and one
  js/banner.js, mounted once at the foot of the document below #contact, with
  the same IntersectionObserver gate and reduced-motion branch that are running here.
