Results

live on the App Store

PantryPal is on the App Store for anyone to cook with, and the store keeps count. Seven people have rated it, and every one of them gave it five stars.

What I learned

it’s all about iteration

Nothing in PantryPal came out right the first time. Every part of it went round the same loop, and stayed open to change until real use settled it.

  1. 1Grow the look in FigmaA visual language has to be grown by hand. The kitchen, its stickers and its chef came from screen after screen.
  2. 2Hand it to friendsPut it out early, rough as it is. Friends find what your own eyes have stopped seeing.
  3. 3Ship it, then listenThe real issues only turn up in real kitchens, on real phones. It shipped as 1.0; it’s on 1.3 now.
  4. 4Change your mindStay open and flexible. When use proves the plan wrong, change the plan, and go round again.

what every lap leaves behind

Cement it in a system, so AI can carry it.

What survives a lap gets written down: the tokens, seventeen components, and a style guide Gemini draws to. Once the language lives in the system, AI does the repeat work in it, Claude Code building screens from its parts and Gemini drawing every food as one of its stickers, and my time goes to the next lap.
